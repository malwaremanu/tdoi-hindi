# Ep 2 — SFX + score plan (cold open, 00:00–01:12)

**Show:** The Discovery of India, Hindi mein · **Narrator:** Asha (voice: Fola)
**Scene:** March 1936. A small plane flies east from Cairo over open desert. Nehru sits inside with a basket beside him. In the basket is a small urn holding Kamala's ashes.
**Style:** Same house style as Ep 1: sparse, dry, literal, documentary. **New for this episode:** the producer wants a restrained score. It is a floor under the grief, not a voice of its own. It is melancholy and minimal: one drone and a handful of single long notes, with no melody and never anything dramatic. Spot effects are almost absent. The engine *is* the scene.

**Level convention:** as in Ep 1, narration is mastered to **-16 LUFS integrated** (peaks ≤ -1 dBTP). "dB under voice" means the sound's short-term loudness relative to the voice's short-term loudness at that moment. In gaps it is measured against the voice's average level. **Producer's rule for this episode:** effects (including the bed) sit **12–16 dB under** the voice. The score sits **2–4 dB below the effects** and is allowed to go quieter, never louder.

**Timing note:** the narration timings are whole seconds. Every ".s" time below is a target placed in a gap. Re-spot each one against the waveform so that nothing lands on a word.

## 2. Score design

**What it is**
- **M1, the drone.** A single low **bowed string held on D** (cello or double bass, around D2–D3, ~73–147 Hz). It should be a real recording, sustained, with slow bow changes and almost no vibrato. It has no rhythm and no chords, so it is heard as a floor more than as "music". Alternative if no good bowed take turns up: a **shruti box / harmonium drone on D**, low-passed hard so the reed buzz disappears.
- **M2, two single cello long notes.** First **A** (00:39.6), then **F** (00:47.6). Over the D drone they suggest D minor without ever playing a tune. Each is one bow stroke of 4–6 s with a soft attack and a natural decay. There are no other notes in the whole open.
- Everything comes from royalty-free or CC0 recordings (see §5). If the drone and the note samples aren't on D, pitch-shift them to D (±2 semitones at most, formant-safe). **Before committing, check the engine's dominant hum in a spectrum analyser.** If it is near D/A, keep D. If it clashes (beats against the drone), move the whole score to the key that sits on the engine fundamental or a fifth above it, and transpose M2 to match. The score must fuse with the engine, not argue with it.

**When it enters:** 00:24.5, as the narration moves *inside* the plane ("अंदर एक मुसाफिर"). The first 24 s are deliberately engine-only: the desert and the emptiness come first, and the man comes later.

**Shape**
```
level (dB under voice)
-18 |                               ___
-20 |            ______________ ___/   \____________
-22 |         __/       [A]               [F]       \
-24 |  ......                                         \  (gone)
     00:24.5   00:28.5   00:39.6   00:47.6  00:53.8   01:04.2 → 01:04.9
```
- 00:24.5–00:28.5: fades in from nothing.
- 00:28.5–00:47.6: holds at -20. The A note sits on the kalash and ash lines.
- **00:47.6–00:53.8: the only swell, +2 dB (to -18).** It sits under the memory of Kamala alive ("वो हंसती थी… हम कहां चलेंगे"). This is the warmest moment, and the music leans in only here. +2 dB is the ceiling. It keeps the score 2 dB under the engine, as the producer's rule requires.
- 00:53.8–01:04.2: back to -20 for the facts (year, month, route, name).
- **01:04.2–01:04.9: drops out completely**, before "और टोकरी में जो है वो कमला है".

**Where it must be gone**
- From **01:04.9 onward.** Kamala's name and the last two lines play over the plane alone, and then over nothing. The music pulling away *is* the gesture. It must never return, not for "घर पास आ रहा है" and not under the host intro.
- It must also never be present before 00:24.5.

## 3. Continuous bed (B1 engine + B2 dry air)

**What it is:** a single-engine 1930s piston/propeller aircraft at steady cruise, recorded or heard from inside a small cabin. It is a warm, low drone with a soft propeller beat. It must not be a jet and must not be a modern turboprop whine. On top is a thin, dry hiss of air passing the fuselage (B2). There is no rattle, no passenger chatter and no cabin announcement.

| Time | B1 engine | B2 air | Movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00:00.0 | -16 | -22 | Fade in 800 ms. The plane is already in the air |
| 00:00.8 → 00:08.0 | -16 | -22 | Steady |
| 00:08.0 → 00:12.8 | **-13** | -22 | Rises in the 5 s gap, so the engine is the only thing in the world. Back down before "इंजन की एक ही आवाज है" |
| 00:13.0 → 00:20.0 | -16 | -22 | Steady, "लगातार" |

| 00:23.0 → 01:07.6 | -16 | -22 | Steady. It does not move for the score. The score fits under it |
| 01:07.6 → 01:09.1 | → E2 (throttle-back), -14 then -16 | -22 | Crossfade into the descent |

The engine take needs to be at least 60 s long, or two takes crossfaded (the 00:20 crossfade can hide the join). No loop point may be audible. Keep B1 narrow (width ≤ 50%), panned slightly left. B2 can be a little wider and slightly right, so the cabin has two sides.

**Fallback for E2:** if no clean "throttle back" recording is found, make it from B1 itself. Apply a varispeed/pitch ramp of -1 semitone over 2 s from 01:07.6, plus a low-pass that eases from 8 kHz to 4 kHz.

## 4. Where silence matters more than sound

1. **00:02–00:12, the sand.** There is no wind, no sand hiss and no music. The emptiness comes from the absence of anything except the engine. In the **00:08–00:13 gap** the engine is literally all there is.
2. **00:16–00:21, "एक घंटा बीतता है। फिर दूसरा।"** No clock, no tick, no musical figure. The only sign of time passing is an engine crossfade nobody will consciously hear.
3. **00:33–00:39, "जैसे कोई बच्चा सो रहा हो…"** There is no lullaby, no baby sound and no second basket creak. The image is the narration's job.
4. **00:44–00:47, "ये राख एक औरत थी।"** The A note has died away and the F note hasn't started yet. The hardest line gets only the drone.
5. **01:05–01:07, "वो कमला है।"** The score has just left. Her name sits over the engine alone. This is the most important "absence" in the open.
6. **01:11.8–01:12.0, before "नमस्कार".** True digital silence: no bed tail, no hiss, no reverb tail. The host intro must be studio-dry.

## 6. Mix notes

- **Levels:**
  - The bed sits at **-16 under the voice**, with one lift to -13 in the 00:08–00:13 gap.
  - Spot effects sit at -14 to -16.
  - The score sits at **-20**, with the one swell to **-18**.
  - Nothing in the score ever reaches the bed level. With everything on, the open's integrated loudness should rise by **≤ 1 LU** over dry voice. The bed is louder than in Ep 1 by design. If it rises more than 1 LU, pull the bed down 1 dB before touching anything else.
- **Ducking:** use the house policy, which is **no sidechain ducking**. Pumping under a steady engine drone is very audible. Automate by hand instead. If a word gets masked (most likely the soft "वो हंसती थी" during the swell), ride the score down by 1–2 dB for that word only, and never raise the voice to compensate.
- **EQ:**
  - **B1 engine:** high-pass 50 Hz. Cut 200–400 Hz by 3 dB (that is where it muddies Fola's voice). Cut 2–4 kHz by 3 dB.
  - **B2 air:** high-pass 1.5 kHz, low-pass 7 kHz, and cut 5–8 kHz by 2 dB so it never reads as extra sibilance.
  - **M1 drone:** high-pass 40 Hz, low-pass 2.5–3 kHz (remove the bow hair and reed buzz). Cut 250 Hz by 2 dB, Q 1.
  - **M2 cello:** high-pass 60 Hz, low-pass 4.5 kHz, cut 1.5–3 kHz by 2 dB, so the note sits behind the consonants.
  - **E1/E2:** high-pass 80 Hz.
- **Space:** everything stays dry and close except the cello, which may keep its own natural small-room tail. **Add no reverb** to anything. A cabin is a small, dead space.
- **Stereo:** the voice is dead centre. The engine sits slightly left (-0.3) and the air slightly right (+0.2). The basket is right of centre (+0.35, beside him). The score is centred and moderately wide, with the cello note nudged to +0.15 so it doesn't sit inside the voice.
- **Timing anchors:** if the narration is re-rendered, re-spot these: the score entry (00:24.5), the A note (00:39.6), the F swell (00:47.6), the **score drop-out (01:04.2–01:04.9)**, the throttle-back (01:07.6) and the bed fade-out (01:08.5 → 01:11.8). These six carry the structure.
- **Avoid:**
  - Melody of any kind: no sad piano, no felt-piano arpeggios, no string-section swell, no choir, no "cinematic" pads or risers
  - Indian-classical clichés used as emotion buttons: no sarangi wail, no bansuri alaap, no tabla, no tanpura cycle under the speech
  - Plane flyby whooshes (we are inside the plane), jet sounds, turbulence rattles, cockpit radio
  - Desert wind howl or sand-blowing effects
  - Clock ticks for "एक घंटा… फिर दूसरा", or heartbeats
  - Baby or child sounds for "जैसे कोई बच्चा सो रहा हो"
  - Any clink or ring on the kalash (urn) lines
  - Any music or sting into, under or after "नमस्कार"
  - Any "1936" filtering (gramophone crackle, vintage EQ)
  - Audible loop seams
- **QC:**
  1. Listen at very low volume on phone speakers. The score should be barely there, and if you notice it before the words, pull it down 2 dB.
  2. On headphones, check that the score drop at 01:04.9 is complete, with no tail under "कमला है".
  3. Check that 01:11.8–01:12.0 is true digital silence.
  4. Listen once with the score muted. If the open still works, the score is at the right level.

## 7. Kya sunayi dega

1. शुरू से आख़िर तक एक छोटे जहाज़ का इंजन लगातार गूंजता रहेगा, बाहर सिर्फ़ रेत है और कोई दूसरी आवाज़ नहीं।
2. जब हम जहाज़ के अंदर नेहरू के पास पहुंचेंगे, तब बहुत धीमे से एक गहरा, ठहरा हुआ सुर आएगा, और टोकरी हल्की सी चरमराएगी।
3. कमला की हंसी और उनकी आंखों की बात पर वो सुर थोड़ा सा उठेगा, बस एक पल के लिए, फिर वापस बैठ जाएगा।
4. "वो कमला है" से ठीक पहले संगीत चला जाएगा, इंजन धीमा होकर दूर हो जाएगा, और नमस्कार पूरी ख़ामोशी के बाद साफ़ आवाज़ में आएगा।
