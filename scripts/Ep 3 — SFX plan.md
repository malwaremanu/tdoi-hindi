# Ep 3 — SFX + score plan (cold open, 00:00–01:38)

**Show:** The Discovery of India, Hindi mein · **Narrator:** Asha (voice: Fola)
**Scene:** A North Indian village, late afternoon, 1936–37. Thousands of people sit in a harvested, hard, dry field. A car comes down a dirt road with a dust cloud behind it. A thin man in a white cap gets out. The crowd stands and chants. He asks who Bharat Mata is, and the field goes quiet.
**Style:** Same house style as Ep 1: a BBC history documentary, not a radio drama. The sound is sparse, dry and literal. The difference this time is a **restrained background score**: one drone instrument, no melody, no percussion. The mood is heat, dust and anticipation, not a rally. Only the cold open gets sound. **From 01:38 to the end of the episode there is nothing under the voice.**

Level convention (as Ep 1): narration mastered to **-16 LUFS integrated** (peaks ≤ -1 dBTP). "dB under voice" means the cue's short-term loudness relative to the voice's short-term loudness at that moment.

**Producer rule and how this plan applies it:**
- **Effects: 12–16 dB under the voice.** Every spot event, and the *summed* bed (B1 + B2), lands inside -12…-16 dB. Single layers inside the bed can sit lower than -16 because they are heard summed. Values below -16 only appear during a fade to or from -∞.
- **Score: 2–4 dB below the effects** that are playing at the same moment. Its ceiling is therefore -15 dB (only at the swell, against -12 dB crowd peaks). Where no effects play (the coda, 01:18–01:37), the score stays at **-20 dB**. It must never rise to fill the space.

## 2. Score design (M1 + M2)

**What it is.** The score is a **tanpura drone** (M1): a pre-recorded royalty-free/CC0 loop of the four strings cycling Pa–Sa–Sa–Sa. It is the most "Indian field, 1930s" sound available without becoming film music. It has no melody, no rhythm and no tabla, and it never resolves. For the swell only, a second layer is added: a **shruti box / harmonium drone (M2)** holding low Sa, a CC0 sustained-note recording. Nothing else is used: no pads, strings, risers or bansuri.

**Preparation**
- Pick the pitch from the loop. Sa should sit **between C and D** (pitch-shift ≤ 1 semitone if needed), below Fola's speaking fundamental. Check by ear that the drone doesn't "hum along" with the voice. If it does, move it a semitone.
- EQ: high-pass at 70 Hz, a 3 dB scoop at 1.5–3 kHz (the Hindi consonant range), and low-pass at 6 kHz so the jawari buzz stays soft.
- Loop: use ≥ 60 s of material or crossfade two takes (2 s equal-power) so there is no audible seam. M1 runs for 33 s plus 17 s, so one clean minute is enough.

**Shape**

## 4. Where silence matters more than sound

1. **01:03 → 01:09.5, "ये भारत माता कौन है?"** This is the centre of the episode. The score and the crowd are both gone by 01:03.0, so the question is asked over nothing but the field. There is no sting, no drone and no "hmm" from the crowd. The audience's silence is the hushed crowd, which is literal.
2. **01:09.5 → 01:15.4, "मैदान में सन्नाटा छा जाता है।"** Even the field goes. There is true digital silence around both lines, with no hiss, room tone or reverb tail. Do not tighten the pauses in the recording.
3. **01:15.4, the chuckle.** One small sound in the silence makes the silence real. Do not double it, widen it or add other laughs.
4. **00:41 → 00:47, the man's portrait.** No footsteps, no fabric and no musical colour for "आंखों में कुछ तेज". The score stays flat and the narration does the work.
5. **00:16 → 00:24, गमछा and the child.** These are "शायद" images. Illustrating them (a baby's cry, cloth) would turn the documentary into drama.
6. **01:37.6 → 01:46, the host intro.** Clean voice, from here to the end of the episode.

## 6. Mix notes

- **Ducking:** Use no sidechain ducking (as Ep 1). Spot cues are placed by hand in the gaps. S6, S8 and S9 overlap words by design. For those, automate the cue down 3 dB for the overlapped word only (S9 is already written as a dip to -15 under "भारत माता की जय").
- **Levels:** The ceiling is **-12 (S9 peak only)**. The bed sits at -14 to -16 and the score at -18 base, -15 at the swell and -20 in the coda. These levels are much hotter than Ep 1's (-26 bed), as the producer rule requires. So check that, with all FX and score on, the integrated loudness of 00:00–01:38 rises by **less than 1 LU**. If it rises more, pull the bed (not the spot cues) down 1–2 dB, staying in the -16 window.
- **EQ / space:** High-pass spot cues at 80–100 Hz. Cut 2–4 kHz by 2–3 dB on B2, S6, S8 and S9 (crowds sit right on the Hindi consonants ख, ट, ड, ज). Add no reverb anywhere. Distance has to come from recordings that are actually distant (S1, S3, S7, B2).
- **Stereo:** Voice dead centre. The road and car sit on the right (R 60 → 20%), and the stage is slightly left. Crowd elements are wide. The score is centred, at 60% width.
- **Timing:** Cue times are on the narration file's timeline (00:00.0 = first sample of "दोपहर"). If the narration is re-rendered, re-spot these anchor points first: the M1 entry (00:28.0), the S4 engine cut (00:37.7), S9 peak and dip (00:53.6 / 00:54.0), the M1 + B2 exit (01:03.0), the B1 exit (01:09.5), S11 (01:15.4) and the M1 hard stop (01:37.6).
- **Avoid:**
  - Melodic music, sitar/bansuri phrases, tabla, "patriotic" cues, and any song with lyrics
  - Risers, whooshes, booms and stings, especially on the question and into the host intro
  - Any intelligible slogan in the crowd recordings ("जय" in the audio would clash with Asha's line)
  - Microphone/PA feedback, applause and stadium crowds: this is 1936, an open field, with no amplification
  - A modern car engine, a horn, or tyre skid
  - Period-radio crackle or vinyl filters
  - Anything at all under 01:38 → end of episode
- **QC:** Listen once on phone speakers at low volume. If the crowd or score is noticed before the words, pull it down 2 dB (staying in the window). Then listen once on headphones and confirm that 01:09.5–01:15.4 and 01:16.0–01:18.5 are truly silent, and that nothing is under the host intro: no score tail, hiss or click.
