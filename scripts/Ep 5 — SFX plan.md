# Ep 5 — SFX + score plan (cold open, 00:00–01:05)

**Show:** The Discovery of India, Hindi mein · **Narrator:** Asha (voice: Fola)
**Scene:** Xi'an-fu, the Tang capital, 7th century. A young Buddhist monk is about to leave for India. At the farewell the Tang emperor comes himself, asks for a cup, dissolves a handful of Chinese soil in it and tells the monk to drink. The monk is Hsuan Tsang. He drinks and leaves. In 629 he reaches India, studies at Nalanda, and goes home with hundreds of manuscripts. Then the narrator turns to the listener with three questions.
**Style:** Same house style as Ep 1 and Ep 3: a BBC history documentary, not a costume drama. The sound is sparse, dry and literal. There is a **restrained score**: two drone instruments, never at the same time except at the single swell, with no melody and no percussion. The mood is court formality first, then road, dust and distance. Only the cold open gets sound. **From 01:05 to the end of the episode there is nothing under the voice.**

Level convention (as Ep 1 and Ep 3): narration mastered to **-16 LUFS integrated** (peaks ≤ -1 dBTP). "dB under voice" means the cue's short-term loudness relative to the voice's short-term loudness at that moment.

**Producer rule and how this plan applies it:**
- **Effects: 12–16 dB under the voice.** Every spot event, and the *summed* bed, lands inside -12…-16 dB. Single layers inside the bed can sit lower than -16 because they are heard summed. Values below -16 only appear during a fade to or from -∞.
- **Score: 2–4 dB below the effects** that are playing at the same moment. Its ceiling is therefore -16 dB (only at the swell, against a -13 bed). Where no effects play (00:52.5 onwards, and the coda 00:56–01:04.4), the score stays at **-20 dB**. It must never rise to fill the space.

## 2. Score design (M1 + M2)

**What it is.** Two pre-recorded drones, both already on disk, used as a floor and never as a tune:
- **M1, low cello drone** (on disk: *cello drone*; *contrabass sustain* as an optional sub-layer, see below). A single bowed low note, no vibrato swells, no phrase. It is the sound of the departure and the road, deliberately not "Chinese": no erhu, guzheng or pentatonic flute, which would turn this into a costume drama.
- **M2, tanpura** (on disk: *tanpura drone*). Pa–Sa–Sa–Sa cycle, no tabla, no voice. It arrives with India in 629, and it alone carries the coda.

The idea is a **handover, not a theme**: cello for the journey, tanpura for the destination, and the only moment both play is the swell when he arrives. No sarangi (too melodic, too emotional for this open), no flute, no pads, risers or percussion.

**Preparation**
- **Pitch:** M1 and M2 must sit on the same Sa, **between C and D** (pitch-shift ≤ 1 semitone), below Fola's speaking fundamental. Tune the cello to the tanpura's Sa (not the reverse: the tanpura's jawari smears when shifted). If the drone "hums along" with Fola, move both a semitone.
- **Contrabass:** only if the cello alone sounds thin on headphones, add the contrabass sustain an octave below at -8 dB relative to M1 (it is part of M1's level, not extra). Skip it if phone-speaker QC shows it only adds mud.
- **EQ:** both high-pass at 70 Hz, a 3 dB scoop at 1.5–3 kHz (the Hindi consonant range). M1 low-pass at 4 kHz (removes bow rosin noise). M2 low-pass at 6 kHz so the jawari buzz stays soft.
- **Loops:** M1 runs 00:06–00:48 (42 s); M2 runs 00:41–00:54.8 and 00:56.2–01:04.4. One clean minute of each is enough; crossfade two takes (2 s equal-power) if a file is shorter. No bow-change bumps: pick a take where the bow changes are inaudible or crossfade over them.

Treat it so it reads as a large wooden hall rather than a street: high-pass 150 Hz, low-pass 5 kHz, narrow-ish stereo. No new "palace" recording is needed.
**B2, the open road (00:38–00:45):** the on-disk **desert wind**. Steady, dry, fine grit; no gusts, no howling, no whistling. Only the plain *wind* file as backup if the desert wind has howls.
**B3, India (00:41–00:52.5):** the on-disk **village afternoon ambience**. Open air, warm, sparse distant birds and insects. Cut any section with voices, engines, horns, temple bells, radio or anything that dates it.

## 4. Where silence matters more than sound

1. **00:54.8 → 00:56.2, "वो देश कैसा था?"** The centre of the open. The bed is gone by 00:52.5 and the tanpura by 00:54.8, so the question is asked over true digital silence: no hiss, no room tone, no reverb tail, no sting. Do not tighten the pause before or after it in the recording. The tanpura comes back only after the question has landed.
2. **00:27 → 00:38, the emperor's words and the name.** No coin clink for "10 हजार पाउंड सोने", no drinking sound for "इसे पी लो", no swell for "हुवान सांग". The bed is at its quietest (the hush) and the score is flat; Fola carries it.
3. **00:14 → 00:18, desert, passes and bandits.** Described, not travelled. Wind here would spend the road bed before the monk has even left.
4. **00:38 → 00:40, "वो प्याला पीता है".** No gulp, no cup. The bed change itself says "he left".
5. **01:02.4 → 01:05, into the host intro.** The coda is gone before the last word ends. Clean voice from 01:05 to the end of the episode.

## 6. Mix notes

- **Ducking:** no sidechain ducking (as Ep 1 and Ep 3). Spot cues are placed by hand in the gaps. The only overlap is S2's ring under "फिर"; if it is audible on the word, shorten the ring rather than lowering the hit.
- **Levels:** ceiling for effects is **-13 summed bed at 00:41–00:43** and -15 for spot cues. The score is -18/-19 base, -16 at the swell, -20 with no effects. Check that the integrated loudness of 00:00–01:05 rises by **less than 1 LU** with everything on. If it rises more, pull B1 and B3 (not the spot cues) down 1 dB, staying inside the -16 window.
- **EQ / space:** high-pass spot cues at 100 Hz (S2, S3 at 200 Hz: they are small objects). Cut 2–4 kHz by 2 dB on B1. **No reverb anywhere**, including on S1: the "hall" comes from the murmur, not from verb on the footsteps. Distance comes from recordings that are actually distant (S5, B2, B3).
- **Stereo:** voice dead centre. The emperor's steps come from slightly left (-0.3 → -0.1); the cup and soil sit just right of centre (+0.1), as if on a table beside the narrator. The road and India beds are wide; the crow is off right (+0.5). The score is centred, width 50–60%.
- **Timing:** cue times are on the narration file's timeline (00:00.0 = first sample of "सातवीं"). The narration timings are whole seconds, so **re-spot these anchors on the waveform before mixing**: the gaps for S1 (≈00:20.2), S2 (≈00:21.8), S3 (≈00:26.3), S4 (≈00:40.1), S5 (≈00:44.3), S6 (≈00:49.1); the court → road crossfade (00:38.0); the swell (00:41.0); the M2 exit before "वो देश कैसा था?" (00:54.8); the coda entry (00:56.2); and the hard stop (01:04.4). If a gap is under 0.4 s, shorten the cue (S1 → one step, S3 → swirl only) rather than letting it touch a word.
- **Avoid:**
  - Anything "Chinese" or "Indian" as colour: erhu, guzheng, gongs, temple bells, conch, bansuri, sitar, chanting monks, Om
  - A melody of any kind, risers, whooshes, booms and stings (especially on "हुवान सांग", "629" and the questions)
  - Coin or gold clinks, drinking/gulp sounds, horses, camels, caravan bells, bandits
  - Court fanfare, crowd cheers or applause at the emperor's arrival: this is a hush, not a ceremony
  - Any recording with intelligible speech, a modern engine or a horn
  - Anything at all under 01:05 → end of episode
- **QC:** listen once on phone speakers at low volume: if the bed or score is noticed before the words, pull it down 1–2 dB (staying in the window). Then once on headphones: confirm 00:54.8–00:56.2 is truly silent, that the cello and tanpura are in tune with each other at the swell, and that nothing (score tail, hiss, click) is under the host intro.

## 7. Kya sunayi dega

1. शुरू में एक बड़े दरबार जैसी धीमी भीड़ की आवाज़ होगी, भिक्षु के आते ही बहुत हल्की सी नीची सेलो की धुन नीचे बिछ जाएगी।
2. सम्राट आएंगे तो सब शांत होगा, दो धीमे क़दम, प्याला रखने की हल्की आवाज़, और मिट्टी घुलने की छोटी सी आवाज़, बस इतना ही।
3. भिक्षु निकलते ही दरबार की जगह रेगिस्तानी हवा आएगी, फिर भारत पहुंचने पर गांव की दोपहर और तानपूरा आएगा, और बस यहीं संगीत थोड़ा सा ऊपर उठेगा।
4. "वो देश कैसा था?" पर पूरा सन्नाटा होगा, फिर अकेला धीमा तानपूरा बाकी सवालों के नीचे रहेगा और नमस्कार से पहले ख़त्म हो जाएगा।
