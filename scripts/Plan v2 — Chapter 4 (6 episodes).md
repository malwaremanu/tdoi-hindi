## 0. What changed, and why

The episodes for book chapters 4–6 were written from `book/chapters/ch04.txt`, a lossy copy of the source: 22,803 words against the real chapter's **30,739 words** (file total; **30,637 words** of body text under the 22 sub-section headings, which is the number this plan budgets from). The rebuild from the Internet Archive full text restored roughly **7,800 words (1.34×)** that the old copy simply did not have — most of section S02 (Marshall on Indus art and religion, Childe on the merchant community, cotton, the destruction of the excavated city), most of S04 and S08 (Vyasa-śruti of the word "Hindi", caste as the peaceful alternative to extermination, the Brahmin class's record), all of S12, most of S22 (the Kalinga, remorse and "all sects" edicts in Ashoka's own words), plus damage repaired in S06 (the **full** Song of Creation hymn is there now), S09 (Schopenhauer's whole sentence, India not isolated) and S20 (the river-and-flame simile, "hatred ceases by love"). Because the old episodes were written at a **0.21× ratio** against a source that was itself a quarter missing, five episodes are structurally too few: the owner's decision is **six episodes at roughly 1.0× ratio — Ep 4.1 … 4.6, about 5,100 source words and 5,000–5,200 narration words each** (≈32 minutes at 160 words/min). The new split puts the extra episode at the seam the old plan had already pre-marked (§2.0: "split at the pre-marked seam between B4 and B5" of the old 4.5) — the Buddha/Maurya tail — and re-cuts four of the five existing scripts around it.

**Set question (R5, unchanged, goes in every script's Episode info header):**
*"Nehru ne Bharat ko 'khoja' — par khoj mein mila kya: ek jaati, ek dharm, ya kuch aur?"*

**Set answer, built up across the six payoffs:**
- 4.1 — a people that absorbs everyone, and raises a wall inside itself;
- 4.2 — a mind that asks questions joyfully, and calls that mind its religion;
- 4.3 — a people that remembers in stories, because it never wrote its own history;
- 4.4 — two epics that drew the first map of one country, from Gandhara to the Vindhyas;
- 4.5 — a working, trading, singing people — and the empire that grew out of their roads;
- 4.6 — an emperor who won everything and then had it carved in stone that real victory is over oneself.

### 1.1 Source-file caveat — read this before opening the source

`book/chapters_v2/ch04.txt` has a **heading-offset defect**: the `## ` heading lines are inserted up to 14 lines away from where the section really begins, the real heading text survives inside the body as a plain paragraph (OCR-flattened: "The Acceptance and the Negation of Life", "Chandragupta and Chanakya. The Maurya Empire Established"), and in places a heading lands mid-word ("…and p" / `## The Coming of the Aryans` / "ossibly even beyond"). Consequences:
- **The Mahābhārata section reads as 62 words if you trust the `## ` blocks; it is really 1,280 words**, and its opening is sitting inside the block labelled "The Bhagavad Gita".
- S13 is genuinely 3,092 words (not 3,413) and S16 3,897 (not 4,339); S22 is 1,314 (not 2,051); S17 1,358 (not 545).

### 1.2 Loading rules for this split

**What may never be split across episodes (R10):**
1. **An anecdote.** Ananda and the dry leaves (4.6-B6); Nehru's mother and the Ramlila (4.4-B2); the 1936 visit to the crumbling Mohenjo-daro (4.1-B4); Megasthenes walking Pataliputra (4.6-B2); the excavation of Ashoka's pillared hall (4.6-B9).
2. **An argument, from its claim to Nehru's verdict.** S07's two streams → the shell (4.2-B4/B5, verdict in B5); S11's individualism → the prison (4.3-B3/B4, verdict in B4); S12's "the materialists' books are gone" → "so we know them only through their enemies" (4.3-B5); S13's "no chroniclers" → "so a woolly mind about facts" (4.3-B7/B8).
3. **A mystery, question to answer, inside one episode.** *Vikram kaun the?* is opened in 4.3's cold open and answered in 4.3-B9. It must not be carried into 4.4.
4. **A character's turn.** Kalinga → the conquest of hearts (4.6-B8) is one beat and one turn.
5. **A quotation and Nehru's response to it.** Schopenhauer's line plus Nehru's comment on it (4.3-B1); Humboldt plus "every school reads it its own way" (4.4-B7).
6. **A planted promise and its payoff** may cross episodes, but only **once per set**: 4.6's cold open plants Buddha's two sayings; 4.6-B4 and 4.6-B8 pay them off. The other cross-episode hand-offs (4.5-B6 Deer Park → 4.6-B3; 4.5-B7 Ashoka's edicts → 4.6-B8) are **T2 facts with a T4 recap line only** — they are not promises.

**Seams of this split (R10 hook type in brackets):**
- 4.1 → 4.2: the newcomers have readable words and a very old book [Arrival / object].
- 4.2 → 4.3: the questions narrowed into a small elect, and the books of the doubters are gone [open question the source poses].
- 4.3 → 4.4: the history was never written, but two stories survived and between them lies a whole country [Promise, the set's one planted promise].
- 4.4 → 4.5: leave kings and gods; walk with a nameless young man to Taxila [Arrival].
- 4.5 → 4.6: an empire stands; near Banaras a man is about to give his first sermon on the day a prisoner in Ahmadnagar remembers him [Arrival + promise].
- 4.6 → ch 5: the Maurya empire crumbles; a Greek envoy puts up a Sanskrit pillar [open question].

**Clusters allowed to be T5 "Kitaab mein aur" only, and nothing else.** A cluster may go to T5 **only** if it is one of:
1. a scholar's or author's **name** whose *content* is spoken (Marshall, Childe, Dodwell, Vincent Smith, Joad, Winternitz, Max Müller, Macnicol, F. W. Thomas, Bloomfield, AE, Gilbert Murray, Bain, Michelet, Humboldt, Rhys Davids, Fick, Stcherbatsky, Barth, Spooner, Wells, Keith, Narada, Strabo, Firdusi, Edwin Arnold, T. E. Lawrence);
2. a **list tail beyond the 3 spoken items** (the ten peoples; the port list; the export list; the six-committee list; the festival-and-entertainment list; the topics of the Arthashastra; the metals, gems, crafts and professions of the bazaar);
3. a **precise figure or variant date** (Winternitz's 2,000–2,500 BC; 1500 BC; 487 vs 544 BC; 9 miles, 570 towers, 64 gates, 600 ft × 30 cubits; 10¹⁸/10⁵⁰; 1/17 second; 302 BC's 500 elephants);
4. a **book or work title** (the Rajatarangini, the Mudrarakshasa, the Arthashastra as a title, *Light of Asia*, Shahnamah, the critical edition).

**Never T5:** a claim, an argument, a causal link, a verdict, an anecdote, or any name the episode's own argument leans on. T5 holds ≤20% of the episode's U atoms.

**The exact rule for when a cluster must be spoken.** A cluster **must** be spoken (T1/T2/T3) in the episode that owns it if any of these is true:
- it carries a **claim or verdict** ("caste began with a hard division", "the world-denying reading is wrong", "the materialists attacked every vested interest");
- it carries a **causal link** the beat's "isliye…/nateeja?" depends on;
- it is one of the **three anchor items** of a T3 list;
- it is a **date or number that the beat is about**;
- it is the **payoff** of the cold open or of a planted promise;
- it answers the episode's **hook-out** line.
Everything else in the cluster (the tail of a list, an extra scholar name, an exact measurement) goes to T5. If a beat's Delivers column marks a cluster "T2" and the draft only gestures at it, that is a coverage failure, not a style choice — the Writer's check must tick the cluster against the Part.

**Numbering and file names (R18, unchanged):** script `scripts/Ep 4.1 Ret ka shehar, nadi ka naam — Script.md`; SFX plan `scripts/Ep 4.1 — SFX plan.md` (cold open only); audio `ep04-1/`. Roman-Hindi script → all-Devanagari narration → tighten → whisper transcript → SFX plan.

### 2.2 Ep 4.2: Charaiveti

- **Premise:** The land's oldest words were wonder and questions, not commands. A doubter in a cell reads the Vedas and the Upanishads, finds fellow doubters there, and then watches those questions shrink into a small elect.

Cut to 1944: a lamp in a cell, a prisoner copying the same questions — and one line of the newly restored hymn, *"Kaun jaanta hai, kaun yahan keh sakta hai?"* SFX: stream, wind in leaves, one bird; all of it stops on "1944".
